Service Disruption Escalation Policy

Internal policy reference for authorised customer-support employees.

Use of this source

This fictional policy source supplies organisational rules and reference information for a learning activity. It does not provide an Agent’s task, role, output format or operating instructions. Those must be designed separately.

Policy purpose and scope

Purpose and scope

This policy sets the information and escalation rules for repeated telecommunications service disruptions.

It applies when an authorised employee receives a report of a recurring or unresolved service problem.

Information to establish

Before selecting a pathway, establish the relevant facts from the customer, available records and approved organisational information.

  • Service and equipment involved
  • Dates, times, duration and frequency
  • Whether all devices are affected
  • Router or equipment indicators
  • Troubleshooting already completed
  • Previous support contacts or case references
  • Known area-service information and customer impact

Policy rules

  • Organise the disruption history before recommending another step.
  • Distinguish confirmed facts from conflicting case notes.
  • Do not repeat troubleshooting already completed without a reason.
  • Identify whether the case has moved beyond routine first-line support.
  • Prepare a technical handover where repeated disruption persists.

Records and referral information

  • Concise incident timeline
  • Troubleshooting already completed
  • Missing or conflicting information
  • Customer-impact summary
  • Recommended next support pathway
  • Structured technical handover

Actions requiring separate authority

  • Claiming to have performed a line test
  • Confirming the technical cause
  • Dispatching equipment
  • Booking a technician
  • Promising compensation

Escalation rule

Refer the matter to an authorised specialist when available information is incomplete or conflicting, this policy does not cover the situation, the matter may involve significant harm, or the requested action requires authority outside the receiving employee’s role.
